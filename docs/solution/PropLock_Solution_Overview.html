1PropLock in plain words

If you have never worked with real estate, payments or AI agents, start here. Everything later in the document builds on these few ideas.

The situation today

Imagine you’re looking for a flat to rent in Bengaluru. You see an ad: “Luxury 2BHK, 2 min walk to metro, fully furnished, ₹30,000.” You call the broker and travel across the city. The flat is empty, the metro is a 20-minute walk, and the rent is suddenly ₹40,000. Then the broker asks for ₹10,000 in cash “to hold the flat”.

Now look at it from the broker’s side. They get dozens of calls a day. They book a visit, drive 45 minutes in traffic, wait at the door, and the tenant never comes. Saying “yes, I’ll come on Saturday” costs the tenant nothing, so many people say it to several brokers.

Both sides have good reasons to distrust each other, so everyone protects themselves, and the whole process becomes slow, expensive and full of scams.

What PropLock does, as two simple ideas

Idea 1: a fact-checker for ads

Before an ad goes live, PropLock compares it with things the broker can’t change:

  • the owner’s agreed rent and deposit (written down by the agency),
  • real travel times from a maps service at rush hour,
  • the law on how big a deposit can be,
  • the owner’s own photos and the GPS location where each photo was taken.

Only ads that pass get a “Verified” badge.

Idea 2: a referee who holds a small deposit

To book a visit, the tenant approves a ₹500 hold on their PayPal account. The money doesn’t move yet; PayPal just reserves it. After the visit:

  • tenant came but didn’t like the flat → hold cancelled, no fee;
  • tenant wants the flat → the ₹500 becomes the token and is subtracted from the first bill;
  • tenant never came → the ₹500 compensates the broker for the wasted trip.
Analogy

Think of a hotel. When you check in, the hotel puts a hold on your card. If nothing goes wrong, the hold disappears and you’re never charged. If you damage the room, they charge it. PropLock uses the same PayPal feature, called an authorization hold, for property visits. The rules for releasing or keeping the money are enforced by software, using proof such as a door PIN and GPS, instead of one side’s word against the other’s.

Five words you’ll see everywhere

2The problem

Getting leads is easy for brokers now: portals, WhatsApp groups and chatbots bring plenty. What’s broken is the middle of the funnel, turning an inquiry into a real visit and a closed deal, because neither side can trust the other.

2.1 The commitment–trust deadlock

Brokers get no commitment

A broker’s stock in trade is their time: driving across town, collecting keys from the owner, giving a tour of up to an hour. A verbal “I’ll come Saturday at 4” carries no penalty if the tenant doesn’t turn up. The PropLock blueprint estimates that up to 70% of free scheduled visits end in no-shows or last-minute cancellations. Brokers are paid only when a deal closes, so every no-show is unpaid work.

Tenants get no trust

Many listings are clickbait: photos of a nicer flat, a “5 min to metro” that is really a straight-line distance, a rent that goes up after the visit, or a flat that was never available. So tenants won’t send token money through a raw payment link to an unknown broker. It looks exactly like an advance-fee scam, and there’s no referee to get it back.

Gap PropLock fills

Each part exists somewhere, but nothing combines claim-by-claim ad verification with a refundable payment hold that releases or pays out based on proof. That combination is PropLock’s core idea.

3The solution

PropLock AI is a trust and commitment layer between brokers, their agency and tenants. Four AI agents each handle one stage of the deal, and PayPal acts as the neutral referee that holds the money.

Core idea

Money is never sent to the broker at booking time. PayPal holds it (an authorization), and the hold is voided (released) or captured (paid) based on proof: a door PIN, GPS presence and both sides’ consent. A small payment becomes a verifiable signal of intent that carries no risk for an honest tenant.

3.1 The four agents

Feature 1 · Trust layer

Listing Audit & Commute-Truth Agent

Checks every ad before it goes live: photos (A), commute times (B), deposit law (C) and the owner’s agreed terms (D). Ads that pass become a signed, verified listing published as a PayPal Catalog product.

Feature 2 · Screening layer

Qualification & Slot-Locking Agent

Chats with the tenant and answers deposit, brokerage and availability questions from the verified listing data. It checks budget, move-in date and profile, then puts a 15-minute soft hold on a visit slot, with travel buffers between visits.

Designed

Feature 3 · Escrow engine

PayPal Auth-Escrow & Check-In Referee

Turns the soft hold into a confirmed visit with a ₹500 / $15 Orders v2 AUTHORIZE hold. At the door it checks a 4-digit PIN and GPS, then voids or captures the hold according to fixed rules.

Designed

Feature 4 · Closing & ledger

Token-to-Invoice Ledger & Dispute Copilot

When a tenant locks a flat, it sends a PayPal Invoice for deposit and brokerage with the ₹500 already deducted. The broker’s dashboard tracks every hold, capture, void and invoice, and answers plain-English questions about them.

4Solution architecture

Two web apps (the tenant’s chat and the broker/manager cockpit) talk to one backend. The backend runs the AI agents, keeps every decision in a database, and is the only part that talks to PayPal.

4.2 Design rules that make it trustworthy

“Unknown” is never “pass”

Every check answers PASS, FAIL or UNVERIFIABLE. A missing API key, a timeout or no map data sends the listing to a human (IN_REVIEW). It never produces a made-up result or a quiet pass.

The AI reads; code judges

The AI model only turns messy text and photos into structured claims, like “2 min walk to Indiranagar Metro”. Whether a claim is true is decided by strict rules. A fooled AI can at worst miss a claim; it can’t award a badge or move money by itself.

Save first, then call PayPal

The signed audit is saved before PayPal is called. A background worker (the “outbox”) then creates the PayPal product, retrying safely with a fixed request ID. A PayPal outage delays the listing; it can’t lose the audit or invent a fake product ID.

Roles and mandates

Only a manager can register flats and give mandates. A broker can list only flats they hold an active mandate for, and their identity comes from their login, so they can’t act as someone else.

In plain words: what is an “API”?

An API is a list of requests one program can send to another, such as “create this listing” or “hold ₹500 on this account”. The PropLock frontends send requests to the PropLock backend, and the backend sends requests to PayPal. PayPal never trusts the frontend directly: only the backend, with its secret keys, can hold, release or capture money.

5Feature 1 in depth: the trust layer

Feature 1 is the foundation: Features 2–4 answer questions from, take bookings for, and invoice against the verified listing it produces. It’s also the part being built right now.

5.1 The four checks

CheckQuestionCompared againstExample result

5.2 What a stored audit report looks like

Each audit stores the full report exactly as it was shown to the broker. Every comparison is listed, including the ones that passed, and the report names the mandate it was judged against. Below is a real result from the current build, for a dishonest submission on the demo flat in Indiranagar:

FieldAd saysOwner allowsResult
rent40000.0032000.00 – 35000.00FAIL
deposit140000.00≤ 70000.00FAIL
furnishingFully Furnished≤ UnfurnishedFAIL
brokerage_fee35000.00≤ 35000.00 (1 month, counted at most at max rent)PASS

Decision: NEEDS_FIX. The broker fixes the ad and resubmits, which creates version 2 with its own audit. Version 1 and its failed audit stay on record as evidence.

5.3 Why it’s built this way

  • Versions, not edits. Changing a verified ad creates a new version that needs its own audit. Old terms stay on record, which protects tenants in disputes (“the ad said ₹35,000”).
  • One transaction per submit. The new version, its audit report and the listing’s new status are saved together or not at all, so there’s never a badge without a report.
  • Signed audit (planned). The verified report is turned into canonical JSON and signed with a server secret (HMAC). Any later change to rent, photos or claims makes the signature fail, and a public /verify endpoint lets anyone check it.
  • Commission is a separate fee, not hidden in the rent. Brokerage is declared in its own field and capped (e.g. one month of rent), so the tenant sees the true move-in cost: rent + deposit + brokerage.
In plain words: LangGraph

LangGraph lets us draw the agent as a flowchart in code: “first read the claims, then run four checks at the same time, then decide.” Each box is a small function. Because the order is explicit, we can test each box on its own, add a new check by adding one box, and be sure the decision waits until every check has finished.

Why an honest broker would want this
  • Paid for no-shows. A tenant who books and doesn’t come forfeits the ₹500, but only if the broker’s GPS proves they were there.
  • Stands out from the clickbait. A verified badge plus a trust score that grows with every first-time-verified listing (+2) and every completed visit (+1).
  • Fewer pointless calls. FAQs are answered automatically, and only qualified tenants with a hold reach the calendar.
  • Protection in disputes. Signed listing versions and PIN/GPS logs prove what was promised and who showed up.
Commission vs. rent markup

Brokers do need to earn. In the normal Indian rental market, commission is a separate fee, not added to the rent: the lease is signed by owner and tenant, so a hidden markup comes out at signing. PropLock therefore allows an asking price anywhere inside the owner’s range and a declared, capped brokerage fee, and blocks rent above the owner’s maximum. Rent-to-rent and co-living operators who legitimately build margin into the rent can be supported later, with the owner’s written consent recorded in the mandate.

8.2 A manager’s week in practice

  1. Monday: two new owners sign up. The manager registers both flats (address, GPS pin, furnishing, a few baseline photos) and sets the rent range with each owner.
  2. Monday: gives Rajesh a 60-day mandate for the Indiranagar flat and QuickCash Rentals a mandate for the Chennai flat.
  3. Tuesday: QuickCash submits the Indiranagar ad at ₹40,000 “fully furnished”. It’s rejected automatically (NEEDS_FIX) with the evidence. The manager does nothing; the system has already explained why.
  4. Wednesday: a listing claims “12 min to Manyata Tech Park”, a route the demo maps table doesn’t cover. It lands in the review queue; the manager checks it and approves with a note.
  5. Saturday: the ledger shows three visits voided (tenants passed), one captured for a no-show (broker GPS confirmed) and one locked, with an invoice sent with ₹500 deducted.

Risks to check before the demo
  • Deadline. Submissions close 12 November 2026. Features 2–4 and the frontend are designed but not built, so the demo should lead with Feature 1 end to end plus a narrow slice of the escrow flow (one AUTHORIZE → void / capture in the PayPal sandbox).
  • INR on PayPal. PayPal stopped domestic payments within India in 2021, so a real ₹500 hold between two Indian accounts may not be possible. Demo in the sandbox in USD ($15) and present INR as the market framing, or name a local partner for production.
  • Authorization timing. PayPal authorizations have a short honor period (3 days in PayPal’s docs) inside a longer validity window (up to 29 days), with re-authorization possible. Visits booked more than a few days ahead need this handled. Check the current docs before relying on the numbers.
  • Legal figures. The deposit caps in Check C are marked unverified until checked against the official texts. Karnataka’s 3–6-month rule is treated as platform policy, not law.
  • Unsourced statistics. Figures such as “up to 70% no-shows” come from the blueprint and should be presented as estimates unless a source is added.

9.3 How it maps to the hackathon

The hackathon has no fixed tracks; its one rule is that a project must meaningfully use both PayPal and AI. PropLock does both at its core: AI agents decide when money should move, and PayPal holds, releases, captures and invoices it. Relevant prize categories include Best Use of Agentic Commerce, Best Use of PayPal + AI and Most Impactful, plus the sponsor prizes for AG Grid (ledger), Bryntum (visit scheduler), APIMatic (type-safe PayPal SDK use) and Render (hosting).
